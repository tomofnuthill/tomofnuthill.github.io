Rockets

Some of the rockets I have built are discussed in this page. They are all designed using chemicals available to me over the counter. While most of these are designed either by myself or with my good friend Ronny, some of them were built as part of the rocketry club we lead at our school

Chlorate Based Propellants (2020 ish)

The first rockets that I built used potassium chlorate as the oxizider. This was because it can be made somewhat easily from potassum chloride via electrolysis. Along with my friend Ronny, I constructed an electrolysis cell using a platinum coated titanium anode. When ran at five volts the cell would produce about 150g of potassium chlorate per week. As potassium chlorate is less soluble than potassium chloride in water as it forms it precipitates out. This means that the chlorate crystals can be collected via filtration.

These rockets involved experimentation with a wide variety of fuels which mainly consited sugar, charcoal, silicone caulking, sulfur. One of the most widely tested propellants contained potassium chlorate, sugar, and silicone caulking as a binder.

Unfortunately, even after 20+ tests a successful chlorate based rocket motor was never created. We had a few that sustained burns during static tests, some even with powerful looking flame profiles, but most exploded. After extensive testing with potassium chlorate it was determined that another oxidizer needed to be used in order to produce a reliable prototype.

Perchlorate Based Propellants (2022 ish)

Potassium perchlorate can be formed by thermal disproportionation of potassium chloride at sufficienty high temperatures. This was done in a ceramic dish over a propane turkey frier. The potassium perchlorate could then be separated from the potassium chloride (the other product of the disproportionation) by recrystalization in water, as it is much less soluble, The reason why this was initally avoided was because it is very labour intensive, requiring about 2 hours of work for 30g. We were very hesitant to scale up due to safely concerns.

By the time we had figured out making potassium perchlorate I had been able to make aluminum powder using a home-made ball mill. This mill was constructed using an electric motor that would spin some skateboard wheels. I modified an old propane tank to serve as the chamber which would be placed on top of the skateboard wheels. The tank was then filled with aluminum foil and some ball bearings which would grind the foil fine enough to burn in air when lit with a match after a week of milling.

As aluminum oxide has a very low heat of formation, aluminum metal is an excellent fuel in a rocket motor because it makes the exhaust hotter allowing for higher exit velocities. The perchlorate propellant used aluminum power as the fuel. It also had nitrocellulose laquer (nitrocellulose dissolved in aceton) to serve as a binder. This fuel had excellent burn characteristics but terrible physical properties meaning that is was incredibly difficult to shape it into the grain necessary for the types of solid rocket motors we wanted to create. We never actually did a full test with the perchlorate based fuel. This was mainly due to how labour intensive it was to make and because we figured out how to make an alternative: potassium nitrate based fuel.

Potassium Nitrate Based Propellants (2022 ish)

Potassium nitrate based propellants are less well performing than perchlorate based ones, however we had by this time figured out how to get nitrate salts. Calcium nitrate fertilizer can be bought at hydroponics stores and it can be reacted with potassium chloride to produce potassium nitrate and calcium chloride. Calcium chloride is very hydroscopic however, so the product required two recrystalizations before it could actually be dried.
